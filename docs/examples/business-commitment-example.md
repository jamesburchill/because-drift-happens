# Business Commitment Example

This hypothetical example shows drift in an ordinary business handoff, without requiring an AI system.

## Intended Commitment

A supplier promises a customer delivery on Friday. The account manager owns the customer commitment; the operations lead owns fulfilment.

## Drift Signal

A later production update forecasts delivery on Monday. The customer record still says Friday, and no one has confirmed a revised date with the customer.

## Evidence and Interpretation

The original confirmation and the later forecast describe different dates. That is evidence of a commitment at risk, not proof that delivery has already failed. The forecast may be wrong, or the customer may have agreed to a change that has not been recorded.

## Authority and Response

The operations lead verifies the forecast and recovery options. The account manager checks the customer record and decides whether to arrange recovery or seek agreement on a revised commitment. A forecast alone does not authorize changing the promise.

## Follow-Through

Record the decision, owner, supporting evidence, and next check. Close the exception when delivery meets the commitment or an authorized revised commitment is recorded. If the evidence is incomplete, keep that uncertainty visible.

## Applying the Doctrine

Awareness reveals the mismatch. Alignment connects the forecast and the promise to an explicit decision. Coordination brings the account manager and operations lead together before a local schedule change becomes a broken customer commitment.
