# Reading Map

Start with the [Drift Doctrine](doctrine.md), then choose the path closest to your work. The doctrine applies across businesses, teams, processes, and technology. The technical framework is one application of it.

## Business and Organizational Drift

1. Read the [Doctrine](doctrine.md) for the six-line premise and its governance response.
2. Read [Drift](drift.md) for changes in assumptions, incentives, policies, and operating conditions.
3. Read [Coherence](coherence.md) for keeping intent, actions, feedback, and accountability connected.
4. Work through the [Business Commitment Example](examples/business-commitment-example.md): a delivery forecast changes while the customer promise stays the same.
5. Practise with the [Guided Exercise](guided-exercise.md): assess a delivery risk, choose a response, and compare your reasoning.
6. Continue with [By Inches](https://vault.jamesburchill.com/content/2026/05/by-inches/) in the Vault for an essay on how small deviations accumulate.

The practical questions are: what was intended, what changed, what evidence supports the signal, and who has authority to respond? A changed plan is not automatically a failure; the response may be to correct execution or explicitly agree on a new commitment.

## Practise Applying the Ideas

After either path, complete the [Guided Exercise: Recognize and Respond to Drift](guided-exercise.md). Allow 15–20 minutes to assess a hypothetical report-delivery problem, write a response plan, and compare it with a suggested response. A changed-assumption challenge and a technical extension help you apply the ideas beyond the initial scenario.

## Content Maturity

Each page identifies its status:

- **Stable doctrine:** the six-line core is the framework’s reference wording. This is a statement of its role here, not a claim of scientific proof or completeness.
- **Working interpretation:** concepts, vocabulary, principles, patterns, and visual interpretations remain open to refinement and evidence.
- **Illustrative example:** a hypothetical scenario for reasoning about drift; not a client case study or production design.
- **Planned work:** a backlog item that has not been completed.
- **Accepted scope:** a recorded framework decision; not evidence that every described capability has been implemented.
- **Reading guide:** navigation and context for the body of work, without a separate claim of doctrinal authority.

Published illustrations can remain working interpretations. Their appearance on the site does not make every visual choice canonical. The [doctrine wording](doctrine.md#canonical-form) remains the reference.
